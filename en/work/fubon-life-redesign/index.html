Project
Overview

Fubon Life's core asset is years of accumulated brand trust, yet after years of incremental expansion its website had grown into a complex information architecture, and the visual design no longer conveyed the professional image the brand deserved. I led the visual optimization and the modular templates of the website, extending the brand's visual vocabulary to set the tone across the site and consolidating modules and templates to ensure a consistent brand experience.

Problem

  • A complex menu structure made content hard to find

    The menu ran up to 5 levels deep, similar content was scattered, and the same products appeared under multiple entry points.

  • Too much jargon made information hard to absorb

    Content was written from an internal corporate perspective, making it hard for users to judge whether a product fit their needs.

  • Visual styles were inconsistent with the group's image

    The website's components and visuals were not aligned with the group's guidelines, leaving it visually disconnected from other platforms under the holding group.

Goal

  • Restructure the information architecture and simplify the menu

    Integrate multiple perspectives and highlight information priority to make content easier to find.

  • Communicate products from the user's perspective

    Use plain, easy-to-understand language so users can judge whether a product fits their needs.

  • Extend the holding group's tone and align with its visual guidelines

    Unify the group's image while delivering brand value through a younger, more approachable visual and verbal language.

How do we integrate diverse requirements into a consistent, easy-to-use digital experience?

Outcome 01

Extending the brand's visual vocabulary to strengthen brand connection

Design is built up through details. I extracted three characteristics from the Fubon logo—circle, square, and overlap—and translated them into graphic motifs and page elements, so the whole site shares one shape language. Building on the existing illustration system of the Fubon Life app, I redrew a set of product scenario illustrations for the website, keeping the original drawing logic while adjusting color layering and detail for the site's larger layouts.

Outcome 02

Modular component design for a consistent corporate visual identity

Following the brand guidelines and design system, I extended new components based on functional needs and defined their types, scenarios, and usage rules, merged into the design system after discussion with the client's team. I also audited templates, consolidating shared modules into reusable design rules to lower future maintenance costs.

Outcome 03

Accessible page design meeting WCAG AA

Following WCAG and the client's accessibility guidelines, I designed dedicated templates for the Financial Friendly Services section around the needs of senior and disabled users, building AA-level contrast standards into the template planning to keep information readable and operable.

Outcome 04

12 usability tests to validate the design concept

Most participants found the new website met their needs, was easy to use, and easy to understand. Testing also revealed that some friction came from visual style and layout rather than features or information architecture, so I iterated on the visual design in parallel.

Take away

Design is not only about solving problems, but reconciling different positions

Even after features and pages are finalized, clients still raise requests to adjust the visuals. Judging by maintenance cost, page purpose, and usability brings the conversation back to the problem itself.

Accessibility should be a default condition, not a final checklist item

In the past, accessibility work on banking websites mostly concentrated on the "Financial Friendly" section. This project convinced me accessibility should be a design default for the entire website.
